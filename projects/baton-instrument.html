The Baton Instrument

An electronic musical instrument that uses tactile controls and physical movement to create and shape sound through a synthesizer.

Music TechnologyHardwareGyroscopesLive Performance

I created the Baton Instrument through Creativitas at MIT to explore how the physical gestures of a performer could become part of an electronic musical interface.

The completed instrument combines four buttons, scroll wheels, and gyroscopes with a synthesizer. The buttons and wheels provide tactile controls, while the gyroscopes introduce motion as another form of musical input.

The idea

A conductor communicates musical ideas through movement. This project began with a related question: could a baton-like object do more than direct musicians and instead become an instrument itself?

The result connects gesture, touch, electronic sound, and live performance in a single prototype.

How it works

Each type of control contributes a different way to interact with the synthesizer:

  • Four buttons provide immediate tactile musical controls.
  • Scroll wheels allow continuous physical adjustment.
  • Gyroscopes turn the movement and orientation of the instrument into input.
  • A synthesizer converts those inputs into music and changing sound.

From prototype to performance

The project brought together hardware prototyping, interaction design, electronic music, and performance. After building the instrument, I premiered it in concert, moving it from an experimental idea to a finished object used onstage.

What the project demonstrates

  • Turning an artistic idea into a functional physical prototype.
  • Designing an interface around both touch and motion.
  • Connecting technology with musical expression.
  • Preparing a technical project for a live public performance.
