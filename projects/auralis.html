Independent AI Project

Auralis

An AI-powered piano transcription and practice project that listens to performances and turns them into useful musical information.

Artificial IntelligencePythonAudio ProcessingMIDIMusicXML

I created Auralis as an independent project at the intersection of piano performance, artificial intelligence, audio processing, and music education.

Auralis listens to piano recordings, detects notes, chords, rhythm, and dynamics, and converts performances into downloadable MIDI and readable notation. The project is also being developed as the foundation for a practice assistant.

From performance to musical information

The core workflow turns an audio performance into information that a pianist can read, edit, and eventually use for focused practice.

Step 1

Listen

Receive a recorded piano performance.

Step 2

Analyze

Detect notes, chords, rhythm, and dynamics.

Step 3

Convert

Create MIDI and readable musical notation.

Step 4

Practice

Build toward useful pitch and rhythm feedback.

Development

I developed transcription workflows for both simple melodies and short polyphonic piano excerpts. The system has been tested and refined using familiar melodies and repertoire excerpts so that results can be compared with known musical material.

The continuing goal is to improve transcription and make the output useful to a pianist, not simply produce raw technical data.

Feedback from Stanford and Carnegie Mellon

Auralis is my own independent project. I brought it to Stanford University and Carnegie Mellon University to work with professors and gather technical and product feedback. I also presented the project to startup CEOs and students for advice about its usefulness, design, and future direction.

What the project demonstrates

  • Applying AI and audio processing to a real musical problem.
  • Combining technical development with knowledge gained as a pianist.
  • Turning audio analysis into musician-friendly outputs such as MIDI and notation.
  • Seeking expert, product, and user feedback during development.
